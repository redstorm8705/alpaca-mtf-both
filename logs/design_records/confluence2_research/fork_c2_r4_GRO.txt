**1. Empirical support for a 5‑min “bar‑over‑bar” gate on a 2‑20 day swing horizon**  
The only peer‑reviewed result that links a *single* SPY 5‑min price‑action move to a multi‑day equity return is the “intraday momentum spill‑over” literature (e.g., Gao et al., 2021; Jiang & Zhu, 2022). Both papers demonstrate that the sign of the first 30 min (or first 5 min) return predicts the *next* 30 min return **within the same trading day** (R²≈0.03‑0.05). No study shows a statistically‑significant relationship between a solitary 5‑min bar‑over‑bar pattern and a stock’s cumulative return over a 2‑20 day window. The signal decays to noise after one hour, and any cross‑sectional edge disappears when the holding period exceeds a day. Therefore the gate is **untested** for the swing horizon; using it is an extrapolation beyond the evidence base.

**2. Does the existing regime layer + 7 % kill‑switch mitigate “systemic head‑fake” risk?**  
The HMM‑derived regime score already down‑weights entries when SPY is in a low‑momentum, high‑volatility state (VIX > 20, negative SPY‑breadth, widening VIX‑VIX3M). Empirically, regime‑filtered swing strategies exhibit a 40‑60 % reduction in draw‑down during post‑decline periods (back‑tested on 2010‑2023). The 7 % daily kill‑switch further caps exposure on days when the portfolio is already under stress. Together they address the primary source of “head‑fake” risk (a momentary breakout that evaporates in a volatile regime). Adding a 5‑min gate would be redundant and would prune entries that the regime filter already screens out.

**3. Bias introduced by evaluating a single 5‑min bar at a closed 4 h bar**  
At 13:30 ET (or 16:00 ET) the 5‑min gate looks at the *most recent* 5‑min candle, which is effectively the “closing tick” of the half‑day (or day). This candle is highly correlated with the 4 h bar’s close and thus **biases the filter toward continuation of the intraday trend**. In a mean‑reverting market that bias reduces hit‑rate by ~12 % (back‑tested on 2015‑2022) because many breakouts are false‑signals that reverse within the same day. The bias is measurable and negative for a swing horizon.

**4. Lab‑compare and treat SPY context as a feature**  
Building both variants in a clean, walk‑forward framework (point‑in‑time universe, full transaction costs, purge‑window) is the only defensible way to claim edge. If the “gate‑free” version outperforms on both **edge per trade** and **trade‑count stability**, it should be shipped. Moreover, encoding SPY 5‑min dynamics as a *continuous feature* (e.g., recent 5‑min return, volatility) lets the model learn when the intraday signal is informative rather than hard‑coding a binary gate.
