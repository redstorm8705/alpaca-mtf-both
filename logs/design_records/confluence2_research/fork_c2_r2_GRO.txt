## (c) Decision model with only a few hundred labeled samples  

1. **Model choice** – Use a **gradient‑boosted tree** (e.g., LightGBM) with **categorical handling** and **early‑stopping**. Trees work well with limited data and can capture non‑linear interactions without heavy regularisation.  
2. **Feature engineering** – Limit to ~30 high‑information features: 4‑h momentum, 1‑d volatility, VIX regime, liquidity bucket, sector dummies, and a few macro‑factors (interest‑rate curve, EUR/USD).  
3. **Training strategy** – Apply **nested time‑series cross‑validation** (rolling windows of 6‑month train / 1‑month validation) to maximise the effective sample size while preserving temporal ordering.  
4. **Label definition** – Binary: “next‑day 2‑%‑of‑capital profit” vs. “not”. Use the **7 % kill‑switch** to discard any sample where the projected position size would exceed the daily entry limit.  
5. **Regularisation** – Set `max_depth=6`, `min_child_samples=10`, `feature_fraction=0.8`, `bagging_fraction=0.8`. This reduces over‑fit risk given the few hundred points.  
6. **Calibration** – After training, fit a **Platt scaling** on the validation set to map raw scores to calibrated probabilities; use these probabilities for risk‑adjusted position sizing.

## (d) Regime layer  

* **Regime definition** – Three regimes: *Calm* (VIX/VIX3M ≤ 1.1), *Normal* (1.1 < ratio ≤ 1.3), *Stress* (ratio > 1.3).  
* **Feature set per regime** – In *Calm* allow higher leverage (up to 2× capital) and include mean‑reversion signals; in *Normal* stick to baseline momentum; in *Stress* restrict to ultra‑liquid names, reduce max position size to 0.5 % of capital, and turn off any beta‑exposure > 0.3.  
* **Implementation** – Regime flag is computed **once per day at market open** from the previous day’s close VIX values; it gates the size‑adjustment multiplier and the allowed signal list for that day.
