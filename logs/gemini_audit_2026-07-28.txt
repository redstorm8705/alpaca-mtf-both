1.  **`midday_audit.py` | `analyse_pnl` | Incorrect P&L calculation for partially-exited positions**
    *   **Description**: In the `analyse_pnl` function, the `qty` variable used for P&L calculation for a full `exit` or `stop_hit` event incorrectly uses the `entry` event's size (`en.get("size")`). This is problematic if previous `partial_exit` events have reduced the position size. The P&L should be calculated based on the `size` specified in the *current exit event* (`ex.get("size")`), which represents the actual quantity being closed.
    *   **Exact failure condition**: `midday_audit.py` line `qty = float((ex.get("size") if ex.get("event") == "partial_exit" else en.get("size")) or 0)` is flawed. For full exits (event is "exit" or "stop_hit"), `en.get("size")` (original entry size) is used instead of `ex.get("size")` (quantity of the current exit). This will lead to an over- or under-statement of P&L for that specific exit tranche in the audit report if the position had prior partial exits.

2.  **`scripts/options_accuracy_evaluator.py` | `evaluate_rec` | Inaccurate MAE calculation**
    *   **Description**: The `mae_pct` (Maximum Adverse Excursion percentage) is calculated using `min(x[3] for x in bars[:peak_idx + 1])`. This approach finds the minimum low price up to the bar where the option hit its maximum high (`peak_idx`). True MAE should track the maximum unrealized loss from the *entry point* throughout the trade duration, not just relative to the peak of the rally. This method may understate the actual adverse excursion experienced by a trade.
    *   **Exact failure condition**: `scripts/options_accuracy_evaluator.py` line `out["mae_pct"] = round(min(x[3] for x in bars[:peak_idx + 1]) / premium - 1.0, 4)`. This calculation provides an inaccurate representation of Maximum Adverse Excursion as it's not consistently measured from the entry point or across the entire risk horizon.
